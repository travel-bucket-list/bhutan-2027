01

The date decision — this is the important one

March and April in Bhutan are two different trips.

Recommendation: mid-March, built around Paro Tshechu. Festivals, culture and nature all fit into 9 days at a level April can't match in the same timeframe. The rhododendrons are the one sacrifice — and you'll still see early blooms on the Dochula–Punakha descent.

All 2027 dates are provisional. Bhutan sets tshechu dates on a lunar calendar (Paro Tshechu = days 11–15 of the 2nd Bhutanese month). Three Bhutanese operators independently list 18–22 March 2027, and that matches the lunar arithmetic, so it's very likely right — but the official calendar is usually published around September–October 2026. Don't buy non-refundable flights until then. One outlier source claims 27–31 March; treat it as wrong unless the official calendar says otherwise.

The Thongdrel — plan for this specifically

On the final day (22 March), the giant Guru Thongdrel — a silk appliqué scroll that covers a three-storey building — is carried in procession from Rinpung Dzong to the Deyangkha festival ground and unfurled at 3:00 AM, then rolled back up before sunrise. Seeing it is said to grant liberation on sight. It is the single most memorable thing in the whole trip and it happens in the dark, in the cold, before dawn. Budget for a 2:30 AM wake-up, thermals, and a nap afterwards. With Ekansh at 5, one parent carrying/wrapping him and being ready to head back early is the sensible plan; Tutun at 11 will be fine for the whole thing.

02

The itinerary — 9 nights, 16–25 March 2027

Nine nights (up from a bare-minimum 7) buys you two things without adding cost anywhere near proportionally: Phobjikha valley slots in, and the old Day 6 headache — Tiger's Nest or the tshechu's best dance day, pick one — disappears because each now gets its own day. Drive times below are real, not optimistic. Bhutan's roads are good but slow and winding; carry motion-sickness medication for both kids.

Day 0Mon 15 Mar · Vadodara → Delhi

Evening flight BDQ→DEL (~2 hrs). Overnight near IGI Airport — a budget airport hotel or retiring room, not a full-service one; you'll be there under 12 hours. You need this buffer — a same-day connection to a morning Paro flight is not worth the risk.

Day 1Tue 16 Mar · Delhi/Kolkata → Paro (2,280 m)

Morning Drukair/Bhutan Airlines flight to PBH (see §4 for routing). The descent into Paro is one of the great landings in aviation — get left-side window seats for Everest, Kanchenjunga and Jomolhari on a clear morning; Tutun is old enough to really appreciate this.

Arrive late morning. Deliberately easy afternoon to let everyone adjust to altitude:

  • Kyichu Lhakhang — 7th century, one of the oldest temples in Bhutan, tiny and atmospheric
  • Rinpung Dzong exterior + the traditional cantilever Nyamai Zam bridge
  • Walk Paro's one-street town — free, and good for tiring out Ekansh before an early night
🌙 Overnight: Paro

Day 2Wed 17 Mar · Paro → Thimphu (2,320 m) · 55 km, 1.5 hrs

  • Buddha Dordenma — 51 m gilded bronze Buddha over the valley; huge hit with kids, free to view from outside
  • Motithang Takin Preserve — Bhutan's bizarre national animal, 15 minutes well spent, nominal entry
  • National Memorial Chorten — the beating heart of Thimphu's daily devotion, free
  • Simply Bhutan or the Folk Heritage Museum — hands-on, good for both Ekansh and Tutun
  • Craft Bazaar for shopping; watch the white-gloved policeman directing traffic (Thimphu has no traffic lights)
🌙 Overnight: Thimphu

Day 3Thu 18 Mar · Thimphu → Dochula → Talo → Punakha (1,250 m) · 75 km, 3 hrs

  • Dochula Pass (3,100 m) — 108 memorial chortens with the eastern Himalaya behind them. Clearest in the morning. Coldest point of the trip — bundle up both kids here especially.
  • Talo Tshechu, final day — a village tshechu 45 min above Punakha. Small, uncrowded, mostly locals, free to attend. A lovely, low-key counterweight to the scale of Paro. (Dates provisional — confirm.)
  • Descend into the Punakha valley: subtropical, warm, rice terraces. A relief after two cold nights.
🌙 Overnight: Punakha

Day 4Fri 19 Mar · Punakha

The best single day in western Bhutan.

  • Punakha Dzong — at the confluence of the Pho Chhu and Mo Chhu rivers, jacarandas and the finest dzong in the country
  • Chimi Lhakhang — 20-minute walk through rice fields to the "Divine Madman's" fertility temple (you'll notice the phallus imagery on the houses; worth briefing Tutun in advance)
  • Punakha suspension bridge — 180 m of swaying prayer flags, free, thrilling and safe — Ekansh will love this
  • Optional (skip to save money): Khamsum Yulley Namgyal Chorten — 1 hr uphill, superb valley views
  • Skip river rafting here — cheaper and just as fun at Phobjikha's nature trail two days later; no need to pay for both
🌙 Overnight: Punakha

Day 5Sat 20 Mar · Punakha → Wangdue → Phobjikha (3,000 m) · 75 km, 2–2.5 hrs

The new addition. A glacial U-shaped valley, the most beautiful landscape in western Bhutan, and — being a farming valley rather than a tourist town — noticeably cheaper for food and stays than Paro or Thimphu.

  • Gangtey Nature Trail — an easy, flat 1.5 hr walk through blue pine forest and open valley, excellent for both kids
  • Gangtey Goenpa monastery overlooking the valley
  • The black-necked cranes have usually left for Tibet by mid-March, so don't build the day around the crane centre — but it's free to pop into if it's on the way

This means skipping Paro Tshechu's Day 3 (the "community day") — a deliberate trade: Day 4 and the Thongdrel, which follow, are the two days that matter most.

🌙 Overnight: Phobjikha — noticeably cheaper beds than Paro

Day 6Sun 21 Mar · Phobjikha → Paro · 145 km, 5.5–6 hrs · Tshechu Day 4

Leave by 7:00 AM. Back via Wangdue, Dochula and Thimphu into Paro by early-mid afternoon. Drop bags and go straight to Rinpung Dzong. Day 4 has the most powerful dances of the whole festival — the Black Hat dance and the Dance of the Lords of the Cremation Grounds — and now it's not competing with anything.

🌙 Overnight: Paro

Day 7Mon 22 Mar · Thongdrel at 3 AM · Tshechu final day

  • 2:30 AM wake, 3:00 AM the Guru Thongdrel is unfurled at Deyangkha ground. Rolled up before sunrise. Free to attend.
  • Back to the hotel, breakfast, sleep until midday
  • Afternoon, gentle and cheap: National Museum (Ta Dzong), last shopping at Paro's craft shops
  • Early night — tomorrow is Tiger's Nest
🌙 Overnight: Paro

Day 8Tue 23 Mar · Tiger's Nest (3,120 m)

No longer competing with the tshechu, so there's no need to rush. Start 7:00 AM, back by early afternoon. 4–5 hrs round trip, ~900 m of climbing. Ponies are available on the uphill leg to the halfway cafeteria only — everyone walks down. Entry Nu 1,000 for adults; Tutun at 11 pays the half-price child rate and is well within age for the full climb. Ekansh at 5 is genuinely too young for the full ascent — the sensible plan is one parent taking him only as far as the cafeteria viewpoint (itself a fine view of the monastery) while the rest continue, or he stays back at the hotel with whichever adult draws the short straw that day. Broken-in shoes, not new ones.

🌙 Overnight: Paro

Day 9Wed 24 Mar · Buffer day, Paro

A genuinely free day, and the cheapest one on the trip — no must-see items, no pressure. Use it for whatever slipped: a relaxed second look at Rinpung Dzong, the hot stone bath (book ahead — the one worthwhile splurge left, and best split between the two families to share the cost), a lazy walk along the Paro Chhu, last-minute shopping, or simply resting before two travel days. If the weather or the kids demand a lighter start after nine days on the road, this is the day that absorbs it.

🌙 Overnight: Paro

Day 10Thu 25 Mar · Paro → Delhi/Kolkata → Vadodara

Morning PBH flight, connect home. Home by evening.

03

Permits, SDF and paperwork

You're travelling as regional tourists, which is a completely different — and far cheaper — regime than the one you'll read about on most international travel sites.

No visa required. You need an Entry Permit.

SDF, adultsNu/₹1,200 per person per night (vs USD 100 for other nationalities) — applies to Bhavin, Sunita, Pradeep, Anandita
SDF, children 6–12₹600 per night — applies to Tutun (11)
SDF, children under 6Free — applies to Ekansh (5)
Permit feeFree
Accepted IDIndian passport (6+ months validity) or Voter ID card only. Aadhaar, PAN and driving licence are NOT accepted.
Children under 18Birth certificate required — for both Ekansh and Tutun
Where to applyOnline at immi.gov.bt (4–5 working days) or on arrival at Paro airport / Phuentsholing
ValidityUp to 15 days, extendable
Route permitNeeded for anywhere beyond Thimphu and Paro — i.e. for Punakha and Phobjikha. Your operator handles this; issued in Thimphu, weekdays only
GuideMandatory for Indian tourists, and required for all dzongs and monasteries
HotelsMust be Department of Tourism–certified properties; booking confirmation is needed before the permit is issued
InsuranceNo longer mandatory (dropped April 2024) — but take it anyway

Do this: get passports for all six, including Ekansh and Tutun. Voter ID works for the adults but a passport is smoother at every checkpoint, and the kids need documentation regardless.

Carry originals everywhere. Permits are checked at every checkpost between districts. Photocopies alone will not get you through.

Note on the SDF discount you may read about: the 50% concession that brings the international rate to USD 100/night runs until 31 August 2027, so March 2027 is inside the window. It doesn't affect you — the ₹1,200 Indian rate is set by a separate bilateral arrangement — but it's the number every article quotes.

04

Getting there — routing from Vadodara

There's no direct anything from Vadodara. Three realistic shapes:

Option A — via Kolkata ✈️ (recommended for this budget-friendly plan)

BDQ → CCU → PBH. Drukair and Bhutan Airlines fly Kolkata–Paro daily, only 1h20m.
~₹26,000–32,000 per person return. Saves roughly ₹30,000 across the group versus the Delhi routing below, for one extra domestic layover — a good trade when the goal is to keep costs down over 9 nights.

Option B — via Delhi ✈️ (more comfortable, costs more)

BDQ → DEL → PBH. Drukair flies Delhi–Paro 5× weekly; Bhutan Airlines also serves the route. ~2h10m on the Paro leg.
~₹30,000–38,000 per person return. Fewest moving parts, marginally easier connections — worth it only if the extra ₹30,000 isn't the deciding factor.

Option C — overland via Phuentsholing 🚗

Fly to Bagdogra (IXB), road to Jaigaon/Phuentsholing (~4 hrs), then Phuentsholing → Thimphu (~6 hrs of mountain switchbacks).
~₹18,000–24,000 per person for the flights — the cheapest on paper, and Phuentsholing itself is SDF-free for the first 24 hours. But it adds a full day each way and 10 hours of winding road with two carsick children. With 9 nights already comfortably budget-friendly via Kolkata, this isn't worth the extra two travel days. Skip it.

Baggage: Drukair economy is generous — 30 kg checked plus 5 kg cabin.

Booking: Drukair releases schedules 3–6 months out, so March 2027 flights will open around October–December 2026. Load factors spike for the tshechu — book the moment schedules open, especially on the cheaper Kolkata route where seats go fastest.

More levers if you want to push it lower still

  • Drop the hot stone bath entirely → saves ~₹8,000 group
  • Skip Khamsum Yulley Chorten (it's already optional above) → saves guide/vehicle idle time, not much cash, but frees up Day 4
  • Simpler homestay-style certified guesthouses in Phobjikha specifically → saves ~₹6,000–8,000 group
  • Cut the buffer day (Day 9) and fly home a day earlier → saves roughly ₹8,000–10,000 SDF/hotel/vehicle for the group, but removes your only slack day after nine days of early starts

Money — read this bit properly

The Ngultrum is pegged 1:1 to the Rupee, so there's no exchange to worry about. But:

  • ₹100 and ₹200 notes are what you want. ₹500 notes are frequently refused outside the big hotels. Break your cash before you leave India.
  • Do not count on UPI or cards. Card acceptance is limited to upscale hotels and shops, usually with a 2–3% surcharge. ATMs exist in Paro and Thimphu but fail often on connectivity, and there's none in Phobjikha — carry enough cash before you leave Punakha.
  • Carry ₹12,000–15,000 per person in cash for meals, entry fees, tips and shopping outside the package.
  • A 5% GST on tour services (hotels, guide, transport) came in from January 2026 — make sure operator quotes are inclusive, not "plus taxes".

07

Why 9 nights, not 7

The bare-minimum version of this trip is 7 nights, and it works — but it forces a choice on Day 6 between Tiger's Nest and the tshechu's best dance day, and it has no room for Phobjikha at all. Two extra nights fix both:

  1. Tiger's Nest and Tshechu Day 4 each get their own day instead of competing — no more picking one over the other.
  2. Phobjikha valley slots in on the way back from Punakha: the Gangtey Nature Trail (easy, flat, 1.5 hrs, excellent for Ekansh), Gangtey Goenpa monastery, and a night in a valley that's noticeably cheaper to eat and sleep in than Paro or Thimphu.

The trade is losing Tshechu Day 3 (the lower-key "community day"), which is the right day to give up if you're only giving up one.

Net effect on cost: because Phobjikha is cheap and the routing/hotel choices in §5 are already trimmed, the 9-night version comes out cheaper per person than a naively-costed 7-night trip, not more expensive.

Altitude

You're going from sea level to 2,280 m in a couple of hours, and up to 3,000 m at Phobjikha. Nothing here is dangerous — Bhutan's western circuit sits well below altitude-sickness territory — but Day 1 is deliberately light for a reason. Drink far more water than feels necessary, skip alcohol the first night, and watch both kids for headaches and poor appetite, especially the night in Phobjikha.

Packing that actually matters

  • Layers, not bulk: thermals + fleece + windproof shell. The 3 AM Thongdrel and the Phobjikha night are both genuinely cold.
  • Broken-in walking shoes for Taktsang. Not new ones.
  • Long trousers and sleeves for all dzongs and temples — this is enforced, not advisory. No shorts, no sleeveless, hats off, shoes off inside.
  • Sunscreen and sunglasses — the high-altitude sun is fierce even at 14°C
  • Motion-sickness medication for Ekansh and Tutun both. Non-negotiable on the Thimphu–Punakha, Punakha–Phobjikha and Phobjikha–Paro legs.
  • Power bank; charging points are scarce at festival grounds and in Phobjikha
  • Basic medical kit: paediatric paracetamol, ORS, antiseptic, any regular prescriptions

Etiquette at the tshechu

  • Arrive early — by 8 AM for good ground positions in the Rinpung Dzong courtyard
  • Photography of the dances is fine; photography inside temples and of altars is not
  • Don't cross in front of dancers or step into the performance circle
  • Locals dress in their finest; smart-casual and modest is the right register for visitors
  • Walk clockwise around chortens, mani walls and temples

Two things people forget

  • Tobacco is heavily restricted in Bhutan. Don't carry cigarettes in quantity.
  • Bhutan is carbon-negative and plastic-conscious. Carry refillable water bottles; single-use plastic is discouraged and in places banned.
