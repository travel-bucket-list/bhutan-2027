March and October are the two best weather windows in Bhutan. March also carries Paro Tshechu, which is why it wins for this trip over an otherwise-comparable October.

June–September is the monsoon — worth avoiding with young kids. Wet trails and slippery dzong courtyards, leeches at lower elevations like Punakha, and monsoon cloud cover that regularly delays or cancels flights into Paro's visual, mountain-ringed approach (there's no instrument landing for the full descent). If dates ever have to shift off March, October is the only real substitute.

At a glance, by season

❄️ Cold and dry. Clear skies, freezing nights, thin winter crowds. Doable with kids if they run warm, but the least comfortable window.

🌸 Spring. Rhododendrons, festivals (Paro Tshechu in March), warming days, cold nights. The best all-round window — this trip's pick.

🌧️ Monsoon. Wettest, greenest, and least reliable for flights and mountain roads. Avoid with young children.

Why the numbers don't quite match the table above. The full-year table is monthly averages sourced generically per town; this one is a trip-specific forecast down to the individual stop, including two places (Dochula, Phobjikha) the monthly table doesn't cover at all. Both are directionally right — trust this second, more granular table for what to actually pack.

What this means for packing

  • Layers, not bulk: thermals + fleece + windproof shell. The 3 AM Thongdrel and the Phobjikha night are both genuinely cold.
  • Sunscreen and sunglasses — the high-altitude sun is fierce even at 14°C.
  • Warmest kit gets used at Dochula Pass and in Phobjikha, not in Paro town itself.
  • Punakha is a genuine relief — pack one lighter layer for the two nights there.
