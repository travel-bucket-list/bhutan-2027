# Bhutan 2027 — Trip Plan
**Travellers:** 2 families, 6 people total, from Vadodara
**Window:** March–April 2027 → **recommended dates: 16–23 March 2027**
**Focus:** Paro Tshechu · dzongs & monasteries · spring valleys
**Planned:** August 2026 (7 months of lead time — comfortable)

> **Assumptions I made.** I've costed this as **4 adults + 2 children aged 6–12**. If the kids are under 6 the SDF drops to zero for them; if 12+, they pay full adult SDF. Everything else scales cleanly.

## 1. The date decision (this is the important one)

March and April in Bhutan are two different trips, and you can't easily have both in 7 days.

| | **Mid-March** | **Mid-April** |
|---|---|---|
| Headline event | **Paro Tshechu, 18–22 Mar** — Bhutan's largest and most spectacular festival | Ura Yakchoe & Domkhar Tshechu (Bumthang) — smaller, more intimate |
| Where | Western circuit: Paro, Thimphu, Punakha — all within 7 days | Bumthang is 8–9 hrs further east; realistically needs 10–12 days |
| Rhododendrons | Just starting at low elevations | Peak bloom; Rhododendron Festival at Lamperi 22–23 Apr |
| Weather | Crisp, clear mornings, cold nights (0–5°C) | Warmer, greener, more afternoon cloud and occasional rain |
| Crowds & prices | Peak — hotels in Paro book out months ahead | Moderate |

**Recommendation: mid-March, built around Paro Tshechu.** Given you picked festivals *and* culture *and* nature, and you only have 7 days, March gives you all three at a level April can't match in the same timeframe. The rhododendrons are the one sacrifice — and you'll still see early blooms on the Dochula–Punakha descent.

### Festival dates in your window (2027)

| Festival | Dates | Location | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chorten Kora | 8 Mar | Trashiyangtse (far east) | No — 2 days' drive each way |
| Gomphu Kora | 16–18 Mar | Trashiyangtse (far east) | No |
| **Talo Tshechu** | **16–18 Mar** | Talo, Punakha | **Yes — bonus, fits Day 3** |
| **Paro Tshechu** | **18–22 Mar** | Rinpung Dzong, Paro | **Yes — the anchor** |
| Rhododendron Week | 3–9 Apr | Merak, Trashigang (far east) | No |
| Domkhar Tshechu | 16–18 Apr | Bumthang | Only on a 12-day trip |
| Ura Yakchoe | 18–22 Apr | Bumthang | Only on a 12-day trip |
| Rhododendron Festival | 22–23 Apr | Lamperi Botanical Park | Only if you switch to April |

⚠️ **All 2027 dates are provisional.** Bhutan sets tshechu dates on a lunar calendar (Paro Tshechu = days 11–15 of the 2nd Bhutanese month). Three Bhutanese operators independently list **18–22 March 2027**, and that matches the lunar arithmetic, so it's very likely right — but the official calendar is usually published around **September–October 2026**. Don't buy non-refundable flights until then. One outlier source claims 27–31 March; treat it as wrong unless the official calendar says otherwise.

### The Thongdrel — plan for this specifically

On the **final day (22 March)**, the giant Guru Thongdrel — a silk appliqué scroll that covers a three-storey building — is carried in procession from Rinpung Dzong to the Deyangkha festival ground and **unfurled at 3:00 AM**, then rolled back up before sunrise. Seeing it is said to grant liberation on sight. It is the single most memorable thing in the whole trip and it happens in the dark, in the cold, before dawn. Budget for a 2:30 AM wake-up, thermals, and a nap afterwards. With kids this is a judgement call, but most families do it and don't regret it.

## 2. The itinerary — 7 nights, 16–23 March 2027

Drive times below are real, not optimistic. Bhutan's roads are good but slow and winding; kids prone to motion sickness will need medication.

### Day 0 — Mon 15 Mar · Vadodara → Delhi
Evening flight BDQ→DEL (~2 hrs). Overnight near IGI Airport. *You need this buffer — a same-day connection to a morning Paro flight is not worth the risk.*

### Day 1 — Tue 16 Mar · Delhi → Paro (2,280 m)
Morning Drukair/Bhutan Airlines DEL→PBH, ~2h10m. The descent into Paro is one of the great landings in aviation — get **left-side window seats** for Everest, Kanchenjunga and Jomolhari on a clear morning.

Arrive ~11 AM. Deliberately easy afternoon to let everyone adjust to altitude:
- **Kyichu Lhakhang** — 7th century, one of the oldest temples in Bhutan, tiny and atmospheric
- **Rinpung Dzong** exterior + the traditional cantilever **Nyamai Zam** bridge
- Walk Paro's one-street town

*Overnight: Paro*

### Day 2 — Wed 17 Mar · Paro → Thimphu (2,320 m) · 55 km, 1.5 hrs
- **Buddha Dordenma** — 51 m gilded bronze Buddha over the valley; huge hit with kids
- **Motithang Takin Preserve** — Bhutan's bizarre national animal, 15 minutes well spent
- **National Memorial Chorten** — the beating heart of Thimphu's daily devotion
- **Simply Bhutan** or the **Folk Heritage Museum** — hands-on, good for children
- **Craft Bazaar** for shopping; watch the white-gloved policeman directing traffic (Thimphu has no traffic lights)

*Overnight: Thimphu*

### Day 3 — Thu 18 Mar · Thimphu → Dochula → Talo → Punakha (1,250 m) · 75 km, 3 hrs
- **Dochula Pass (3,100 m)** — 108 memorial chortens with the eastern Himalaya behind them. Clearest in the morning. Coldest point of the trip.
- **Talo Tshechu, final day** — a village tshechu 45 min above Punakha. Small, uncrowded, mostly locals. A lovely counterweight to the scale of Paro. *(Dates provisional — confirm.)*
- Descend into the Punakha valley: subtropical, warm, rice terraces. A relief after two cold nights.

*Overnight: Punakha*

### Day 4 — Fri 19 Mar · Punakha
The best single day in western Bhutan.
- **Punakha Dzong** — at the confluence of the Pho Chhu and Mo Chhu rivers, jacarandas and the finest dzong in the country
- **Chimi Lhakhang** — 20-minute walk through rice fields to the "Divine Madman's" fertility temple (you'll notice the phallus imagery on the houses; worth briefing the kids)
- **Punakha suspension bridge** — 180 m of swaying prayer flags, thrilling and safe
- Optional: **Khamsum Yulley Namgyal Chorten** — 1 hr uphill, superb valley views
- Optional: **river rafting** on the Mo Chhu, gentle Grade I–II, suitable for children

*Overnight: Punakha*

### Day 5 — Sat 20 Mar · Punakha → Paro · 125 km, 4.5–5 hrs · **Tshechu Day 3**
Leave by 7:30 AM. Back over Dochula, past Thimphu, into Paro by early afternoon. Drop bags and go straight to **Rinpung Dzong for the afternoon session** of masked dances. Day 3 is the community day — the courtyard fills with locals in their best kira and gho, and it's the most human of the five days.

*Overnight: Paro*

### Day 6 — Sun 21 Mar · Tiger's Nest (3,120 m) *or* **Tshechu Day 4**
**This is the one real conflict in the itinerary.** Day 4 of the tshechu has the most powerful dances — the Black Hat dance and the Dance of the Lords of the Cremation Grounds. Taktsang takes most of a day.

- **Option A (recommended): Tiger's Nest.** Start 7:00 AM, back by 1:00 PM. 4–5 hrs round trip, ~900 m of climbing. Ponies are available on the uphill leg to the halfway cafeteria only — everyone walks down. Entry Nu 1,000 per adult, half price for ages 6–17. The monastery admits visitors 9 AM–12 noon and 2–5 PM, so an early start gets you inside comfortably. Manageable for fit children over 8; younger ones can turn back at the cafeteria viewpoint, which is itself a fine view of the monastery. Then catch the tail of the afternoon tshechu session if anyone still has legs.
- **Option B:** Full tshechu day, and shift Taktsang to Day 7 morning — but Day 7 starts at 2:30 AM for the Thongdrel, so this is genuinely hard.
- **Option C:** Add two nights (see §7) and stop having to choose.

*Overnight: Paro — early night*

### Day 7 — Mon 22 Mar · **Thongdrel at 3 AM** · Tshechu final day
- **2:30 AM** wake, **3:00 AM** the Guru Thongdrel is unfurled at Deyangkha ground. Rolled up before sunrise.
- Back to the hotel, breakfast, sleep until midday
- Afternoon, gentle: **National Museum (Ta Dzong)**, a **traditional hot stone bath** (book ahead — it's the perfect end to the trip), last shopping
- Pack

*Overnight: Paro*

### Day 8 — Tue 23 Mar · Paro → Delhi → Vadodara
Morning PBH→DEL, connect to BDQ. Home by evening.

## 3. Permits, SDF and paperwork

You're travelling as **regional tourists**, which is a completely different — and far cheaper — regime than the one you'll read about on most international travel sites.

**No visa required.** You need an **Entry Permit**.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SDF, adults** | **Nu/₹1,200 per person per night** (vs USD 100 for other nationalities) |
| **SDF, children 6–12** | ₹600 per night |
| **SDF, children under 6** | Free |
| **Permit fee** | Free |
| **Accepted ID** | Indian **passport** (6+ months validity) **or Voter ID card only**. **Aadhaar, PAN and driving licence are NOT accepted.** |
| **Children under 18** | Birth certificate required |
| **Where to apply** | Online at **immi.gov.bt** (4–5 working days) or on arrival at Paro airport / Phuentsholing |
| **Validity** | Up to 15 days, extendable |
| **Route permit** | Needed for anywhere beyond Thimphu and Paro — i.e. for Punakha. Your operator handles this; issued in Thimphu, weekdays only |
| **Guide** | **Mandatory** for Indian tourists, and required for all dzongs and monasteries |
| **Hotels** | Must be Department of Tourism–certified properties; booking confirmation is needed before the permit is issued |
| **Insurance** | No longer mandatory (dropped April 2024) — but take it anyway |

**Do this:** get passports for all six, including the children. Voter ID works for the adults but a passport is smoother at every checkpoint, and the kids need documentation regardless.

**Carry originals everywhere.** Permits are checked at every checkpost between districts. Photocopies alone will not get you through.

**Note on the SDF discount you may read about:** the 50% concession that brings the international rate to USD 100/night runs until **31 August 2027**, so March 2027 is inside the window. It doesn't affect you — the ₹1,200 Indian rate is set by a separate bilateral arrangement — but it's the number every article quotes.

## 4. Getting there — routing from Vadodara

There's no direct anything from Vadodara. Three realistic shapes:

### Option A — via Delhi ✈️ *(recommended)*
BDQ → DEL → PBH. Drukair flies Delhi–Paro **5× weekly**; Bhutan Airlines also serves the route. ~2h10m on the Paro leg.
**~₹30,000–38,000 per person return.** Most comfortable, fewest moving parts, best for kids.

### Option B — via Kolkata ✈️ *(cheapest by air)*
BDQ → CCU → PBH. Drukair and Bhutan Airlines fly Kolkata–Paro **daily**, only 1h20m.
**~₹26,000–32,000 per person return.** Saves roughly ₹30,000 across the group; costs you a longer domestic leg.

### Option C — overland via Phuentsholing 🚗
Fly to **Bagdogra (IXB)**, road to Jaigaon/Phuentsholing (~4 hrs), then Phuentsholing → Thimphu (~6 hrs of mountain switchbacks).
**~₹18,000–24,000 per person** for the flights — genuinely cheaper, and Phuentsholing itself is SDF-free for the first 24 hours. **But it adds a full day each way and 10 hours of winding road with two carsick children.** I'd skip it.

**Baggage:** Drukair economy is generous — 30 kg checked plus 5 kg cabin.

**Booking:** Drukair releases schedules 3–6 months out, so **March 2027 flights will open around October–December 2026**. Load factors spike for the tshechu.

## 5. Budget — 6 people, 7 nights

Mid-range: certified 3-star hotels, private 12-seater with driver, licensed guide, most meals.

| Line item | Basis | Group total (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| International + domestic flights | via Delhi, 6 pax | 1,80,000 – 2,28,000 |
| **SDF** | 4 adults × ₹1,200 × 7 + 2 kids × ₹600 × 7 | **42,000** |
| Hotels | 2 triple rooms × 7 nights @ ₹5,000–7,000 | 70,000 – 98,000 |
| Vehicle + driver + fuel | Toyota Hiace/Coaster, 8 days @ ₹6,000–8,000 | 48,000 – 64,000 |
| Licensed guide | 8 days @ ₹1,500–2,500 | 12,000 – 20,000 |
| Meals | ₹800–1,200 pp/day (many hotels include B&D) | 38,000 – 58,000 |
| Entry fees | Taktsang Nu 1,000 pp (kids 6–17 half), museums, dzongs | 8,000 – 12,000 |
| Hot stone bath, rafting, ponies | optional extras | 12,000 – 20,000 |
| 5% GST on tour services | hotels + guide + vehicle, from Jan 2026 | 6,500 – 9,000 |
| Shopping & contingency | | 25,000 – 40,000 |
| **TOTAL** | | **₹4,41,500 – 5,91,000** |
| **Per person** | | **₹74,000 – 98,500** |

**Levers if you want to bring this down:**
- Route via **Kolkata** instead of Delhi → saves ~₹30,000 group
- Simpler certified guesthouses instead of 3-star → saves ~₹25,000 group
- Drop one Punakha night → saves ₹7,200 SDF + a room night
- Skip the hot stone bath / rafting extras → saves ~₹15,000 group

**Money — read this bit properly.** The Ngultrum is pegged 1:1 to the Rupee, so there's no exchange to worry about. But:
- **₹100 and ₹200 notes are what you want.** ₹500 notes are frequently refused outside the big hotels. Break your cash *before* you leave India.
- **Do not count on UPI or cards.** Card acceptance is limited to upscale hotels and shops, usually with a 2–3% surcharge. ATMs exist in Paro and Thimphu but fail often on connectivity.
- Carry **₹15,000–20,000 per person in cash** for meals, entry fees, tips and shopping outside the package.
- **A 5% GST on tour services** (hotels, guide, transport) came in from January 2026 — make sure operator quotes are inclusive, not "plus taxes".

## 7. Worth considering: add two nights

If you can stretch to **9 nights (16–25 March)**, two things get materially better:

1. **The Day 6 conflict disappears** — Tiger's Nest and the full Tshechu Day 4 both fit.
2. **Phobjikha valley** slots in: a glacial U-shaped valley at 3,000 m, the Gangtey Nature Trail (an easy 1.5 hr walk, excellent with kids), Gangtey Goenpa monastery, and the black-necked crane centre. The cranes have usually left for Tibet by mid-March, but the valley is the most beautiful landscape in western Bhutan regardless.

Cost of the extension: roughly **₹12,000 SDF + ₹30,000–40,000 everything else** for the group. It's the best value-per-rupee decision available in this plan.

## 8. Practical notes

**Weather, mid-March**

| Place | Altitude | Day | Night |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paro | 2,280 m | 13–17°C | 0–4°C |
| Thimphu | 2,320 m | 14–18°C | 2–5°C |
| Dochula Pass | 3,100 m | 5–10°C | below freezing |
| Punakha | 1,250 m | 20–25°C | 10–13°C |

Mornings are clear, afternoons often cloud over. Occasional rain by late March.

**Altitude.** You're going from sea level to 2,280 m in two hours. Nothing here is dangerous — Bhutan's western circuit sits well below altitude-sickness territory — but Day 1 is deliberately light for a reason. Drink far more water than feels necessary, skip alcohol the first night, and watch the kids for headaches and poor appetite.

**Packing that actually matters**
- Layers, not bulk: thermals + fleece + windproof shell. The 3 AM Thongdrel is genuinely cold.
- Broken-in walking shoes for Taktsang. Not new ones.
- **Long trousers and sleeves for all dzongs and temples** — this is enforced, not advisory. No shorts, no sleeveless, hats off, shoes off inside.
- Sunscreen and sunglasses — the high-altitude sun is fierce even at 14°C
- Motion-sickness medication. Non-negotiable on the Thimphu–Punakha and Punakha–Paro legs.
- Power bank; charging points are scarce at festival grounds
- Basic medical kit: paediatric paracetamol, ORS, antiseptic, any regular prescriptions

**Etiquette at the tshechu**
- Arrive early — by 8 AM for good ground positions in the Rinpung Dzong courtyard
- Photography of the dances is fine; photography **inside** temples and of altars is not
- Don't cross in front of dancers or step into the performance circle
- Locals dress in their finest; smart-casual and modest is the right register for visitors
- Walk clockwise around chortens, mani walls and temples

**Two things people forget**
- **Tobacco is heavily restricted** in Bhutan. Don't carry cigarettes in quantity.
- Bhutan is **carbon-negative and plastic-conscious**. Carry refillable water bottles; single-use plastic is discouraged and in places banned.
